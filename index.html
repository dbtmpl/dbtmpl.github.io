About

Hi there! I'm David, a machine learning engineer at TomTom. For the past six-plus years I've been building large-scale perception and mapping systems and the MLOps pipelines behind them. Today my models detect traffic signs and traffic lights across Europe and North America. I've worked on pretty much every stage of the ML lifecycle: data ingestion, ground truth, training, distributed inference, evaluation, and getting models promoted and released. I also automated release processes, which cut the time to fix production failures from days to hours, and built agentic GIS tooling that makes 50+ engineers around me more productive.

My roots are in computer vision research. I did a research master's in Artificial Intelligence at the University of Amsterdam and wrote my thesis on partially supervised instance segmentation with TomTom's autonomous driving team, which turned into a first-author paper at ICCV 2021. Before that, I studied Human-Computer Interaction in Hamburg and spent a lot of time with robots and VR headsets.

I'm happiest where applied science, ML engineering and software engineering meet: taking an ambiguous problem across research, product and engineering teams all the way to a shipped system (which earned me an org-level leadership award). Lately I've been building coding agents that run our core processes locally and in ephemeral environments, check their results in GIS sessions, and iterate on problems on their own. I'm also part of TomTom's cross-org AI mentoring group.

I'm drawn to problems where new ML research has to become a dependable product, especially in perception and generative modelling. I'm also very excited about efficient computer vision that runs in real time on edge devices, and about physics-based machine learning. These days I'm especially curious about diffusion models seen through the lens of statistical physics: rare events, transition paths, and the thermodynamics of learning.

If you want to talk, feel free to reach out by email: david (dot) biertimpel (at) protonmail (dot) com.
